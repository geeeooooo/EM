SESSION A / PROBLEM 1 / 30 min / 10 POINTS

Maxwell–Ampère law: charging capacitor

QUESTION

A parallel-plate capacitor has two circular plates of radius \(a\) separated by a small gap \(d\ll a\). A constant conduction current \(I\) charges the capacitor. Neglect fringing fields and assume a uniform axial electric field \(\mathbf E=E(t)\hat{\mathbf z}\). Initially the capacitor is uncharged.

  1. Find \(E(t)\) in terms of \(I,a,\varepsilon_0,t\).
  2. Using a circular Amperian loop of radius \(r\), derive \(B_\phi(r)\) for \(r<a\) and \(r>a\) in the gap.
  3. Explain why displacement current is essential, using two surfaces with the same boundary curve.
  4. Give two assumptions behind the approximation.
Reveal worked solution · Parts (a)–(d)

(a) Electric field between the plates

The plate area is \(A=\pi a^2\,[\mathrm{m^2}]\). Constant current \(I\,[\mathrm A]\) deposits charge \(Q(t)=It\,[\mathrm C]\). Gauss's law gives \(E=Q/(\varepsilon_0 A)\):

\[\boxed{\mathbf E(t)=\frac{It}{\varepsilon_0\pi a^2}\hat{\mathbf z}},\qquad \frac{dE}{dt}=\frac{I}{\varepsilon_0\pi a^2}.\]

(b) Magnetic field in the capacitor gap

There is no conduction current through a surface across the vacuum gap. Maxwell–Ampère's integral law instead reads

\[\oint_{\mathcal C}\mathbf B\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell =\mu_0I_{\rm cond,encl}+ \mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}.\]

Cylindrical symmetry makes \(\mathbf B=B_\phi(r)\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\) and gives \(\oint\mathbf B\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=2\pi rB_\phi(r)\).

For \(0\le r<a\): only the circular area \(\pi r^2\) contributes to electric flux:

\[\Phi_E=E\pi r^2,\qquad 2\pi rB_\phi=\mu_0\varepsilon_0\pi r^2\frac{dE}{dt} =\mu_0I\frac{r^2}{a^2}.\]

For \(r>a\): in the approximation of negligible fringing, the field occupies only the plate area \(\pi a^2\):

\[\Phi_E=E\pi a^2,\qquad 2\pi rB_\phi=\mu_0\varepsilon_0\pi a^2\frac{dE}{dt}=\mu_0I.\]

Final result

\[\boxed{B_\phi(r)=\begin{cases} \dfrac{\mu_0Ir}{2\pi a^2},&0\le r<a,\\[6pt] \dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r},&r>a. \end{cases}}\]

Field direction: \(+\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\), given an effective current along \(+\hat{\mathbf z}\) and the right-hand rule.

(c) Surface independence and displacement current

A surface crossing the wire encloses conduction current \(I\). Another surface with the same circular boundary, bulged into the gap, encloses zero conduction current. Ordinary Ampère's law would give contradictory circulations. Maxwell's displacement current \(I_d=\varepsilon_0d\Phi_E/dt\) gives \(I_d=I\) for the full plate area, so both surfaces agree.

(d) Approximations

Use \(d\ll a\), negligible edge/fringing fields, quasistatic/slow charging (negligible radiation), and approximate axial symmetry. Near the plate edges, these expressions are not exact.

SI check: \(E\,[\mathrm{V/m}]\), \(B_\phi\,[\mathrm T]\), \(I\,[\mathrm A]\), \(a,d,r\,[\mathrm m]\), \(\varepsilon_0\,[\mathrm{F/m}]\), \(\mu_0\,[\mathrm{H/m}]\). For example, \([\mu_0Ir/a^2]=\mathrm{T}\).

SESSION A / PROBLEM 2 / 30 min / 10 POINTS

Continuity equation and Poynting theorem

QUESTION

In a stationary, linear, isotropic, nondispersive medium, \(\mathbf D=\varepsilon\mathbf E\), \(\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf H\), and free current density is \(\mathbf J_f\). Begin with Maxwell's equations in differential form.

  1. Derive \(\partial_t\rho_f+\nabla\cdot\mathbf J_f=0\).
  2. Derive \(\partial_tu+\nabla\cdot\mathbf S=-\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf E\), identifying \(u,\mathbf S\).
  3. For \(\mathbf J_f=\sigma\mathbf E\), \(\mathbf E(t)=E_0e^{-t/\tau}\hat{\mathbf x}\), and \(\mathbf B=\mathbf0\), determine \(\tau\) and verify the energy equation.
  4. Interpret the sign of \(\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf E\).
Reveal worked solution · Parts (a)–(d)

(a) Charge-continuity equation

Apply divergence to \(\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J_f+\partial_t\mathbf D\). The divergence of any curl vanishes:

\[0=\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf H) =\nabla\cdot\mathbf J_f+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf D).\]

Gauss's law \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_f\) then gives

\[\boxed{\frac{\partial\rho_f}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf J_f=0}.\]

Positive outward current divergence means free charge density decreases locally.

(b) Poynting's theorem

Use the identity

\[\nabla\cdot(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H) =\mathbf H\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf E) -\mathbf E\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf H).\]

Insert Faraday's law and Ampère–Maxwell:

\[\nabla\cdot(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H) =-\mathbf H\cdot\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t} -\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf J_f -\mathbf E\cdot\frac{\partial\mathbf D}{\partial t}.\]

For constant scalar \(\varepsilon\) and \(\mu\),

\[\mathbf E\cdot\partial_t\mathbf D =\partial_t\!\left(\tfrac12\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf D\right),\quad \mathbf H\cdot\partial_t\mathbf B =\partial_t\!\left(\tfrac12\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf H\right).\]

Define energy density \(u\) and energy flux \(\mathbf S\):

\[\boxed{u=\tfrac12(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf D+\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf H)},\qquad \boxed{\mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H}.\]

Rearrange:

\[\boxed{\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf S =-\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf E}.\]

(c) Field relaxation in an Ohmic medium

For \(\mathbf B=\mathbf0\), \(\mathbf H=\mathbf0\), spatially uniform \(\mathbf E(t)\), and \(\mathbf J_f=\sigma\mathbf E\), Ampère–Maxwell becomes

\[0=\sigma\mathbf E+\varepsilon\frac{d\mathbf E}{dt} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{d\mathbf E}{dt}=-\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\mathbf E.\]

Compare with \(dE/dt=-E/\tau\):

\[\boxed{\tau=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}}.\]

The field energy is \(u=\frac12\varepsilon E^2\). Differentiate:

\[\frac{du}{dt}=\varepsilon E\frac{dE}{dt} =-\sigma E^2.\]

Since \(\mathbf H=\mathbf0\), \(\mathbf S=\mathbf0\), and \(\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf E=\sigma E^2\), one obtains

\[\boxed{\frac{du}{dt}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf S=-\sigma E^2 =-\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf E}.\]

(d) Physical meaning

For a passive Ohmic material, \(\sigma\ge0\), so \(\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf E=\sigma E^2\ge0\): the field loses energy, transferred to the material as Joule heat.

SI check: \(\rho_f\,[\mathrm{C/m^3}]\), \(\mathbf J_f\,[\mathrm{A/m^2}]\), \(u\,[\mathrm{J/m^3}]\), \(\mathbf S\,[\mathrm{W/m^2}]\), \(\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf E\,[\mathrm{W/m^3}]\), \(\varepsilon\,[\mathrm{F/m}]\), \(\sigma\,[\mathrm{S/m}]\), and \(\tau=\varepsilon/\sigma\,[\mathrm{s}]\).

SESSION B / PROBLEM 3 / 30 min / 10 POINTS

Dielectric interface

QUESTION

A planar boundary at \(z=0\) separates linear dielectrics. Below (\(z<0\)), \(\varepsilon_1=2\varepsilon_0\); above (\(z>0\)), \(\varepsilon_2=5\varepsilon_0\). There is no free surface charge. Immediately below the interface,

\[\mathbf E_1=(3\hat{\mathbf x}+4\hat{\mathbf z})\ \mathrm{V/m}.\]

Take \(\hat{\mathbf n}=+\hat{\mathbf z}\) from region 1 to region 2.

  1. Derive the \(E_t\) and \(D_n\) conditions using Maxwell's integral equations.
  2. Find \(\mathbf E_2\), verifying the continuous components.
  3. Determine the net bound surface charge from \(\mathbf P_i=(\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_0)\mathbf E_i\).
  4. Explain why \(E_n\) can jump even when \(\sigma_f=0\).
Reveal worked solution · Parts (a)–(d)

(a) Interface laws

Apply Faraday's static integral law to a vanishing rectangular loop spanning the interface:

\[\oint\mathbf E\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{\hat{\mathbf n}\times(\mathbf E_2-\mathbf E_1)=\mathbf0}.\]

Apply Gauss's law for \(\mathbf D\) to a vanishing Gaussian pillbox:

\[\oint\mathbf D\cdot d\mathbf a=Q_{f,\rm enc} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot(\mathbf D_2-\mathbf D_1)=\sigma_f=0}.\]

(b) Determine the field above

From tangential \(\mathbf E\) continuity, \(E_{2x}=E_{1x}=3\ \mathrm{V/m}\). Normal \(\mathbf D\) continuity requires

\[\varepsilon_2E_{2z}=\varepsilon_1E_{1z} \quad\Rightarrow\quad E_{2z}=\frac{\varepsilon_1}{\varepsilon_2}(4\ \mathrm{V/m}) =\frac25(4\ \mathrm{V/m})=1.6\ \mathrm{V/m}.\] \[\boxed{\mathbf E_2=(3\hat{\mathbf x}+1.6\hat{\mathbf z})\ \mathrm{V/m}}.\]

Check:

\[D_{1z}=2\varepsilon_0(4\ \mathrm{V/m}) =8\varepsilon_0\ \mathrm{V/m} =D_{2z}=5\varepsilon_0(1.6\ \mathrm{V/m}).\]

Each \(D_z\) is in \(\mathrm{C/m^2}\).

(c) Bound charge on the two sides

Polarization is \(\mathbf P_i=(\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_0)\mathbf E_i\). The outward normal of region 1 is \(+\hat{\mathbf z}\), but that of region 2 is \(-\hat{\mathbf z}\):

\[\sigma_b=\mathbf P_1\cdot\hat{\mathbf z} +\mathbf P_2\cdot(-\hat{\mathbf z}) =P_{1z}-P_{2z}.\] \[P_{1z}=(2\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_0)(4\ \mathrm{V/m}) =4\varepsilon_0\ \mathrm{V/m},\] \[P_{2z}=(5\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_0)(1.6\ \mathrm{V/m}) =6.4\varepsilon_0\ \mathrm{V/m}.\] \[\boxed{\sigma_b=-2.4\varepsilon_0\ (1\ \mathrm{V/m}) \approx-2.13\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{C/m^2}}.\]

(d) Physical interpretation

Zero free surface charge requires \(D_{2n}=D_{1n}\), not \(E_{2n}=E_{1n}\). Because \(D_n=\varepsilon E_n\), different permittivities generally give different normal electric fields.

SI check: \(\mathbf E\,[\mathrm{V/m}]\), \(\mathbf D,\mathbf P,\sigma_b\,[\mathrm{C/m^2}]\), \(\varepsilon_i\,[\mathrm{F/m}]\); \(\varepsilon_0(\mathrm{V/m})=\mathrm{C/m^2}\).

SESSION B / PROBLEM 4 / 30 min / 10 POINTS

Magnetic interface and free surface current

QUESTION

At \(z=0\), medium 1 (\(z<0\)) has \(\mu_1=2\mu_0\) and medium 2 (\(z>0\)) has \(\mu_2=4\mu_0\). The magnetic flux density below is

\[\mathbf B_1=(0.60\hat{\mathbf x}+0.80\hat{\mathbf z})\ \mathrm{T}.\]

Take the interface normal \(\hat{\mathbf n}=\hat{\mathbf z}\).

  1. Derive the boundary conditions for \(B_n\) and \(H_t\) from the static Maxwell equations.
  2. Determine \(\mathbf B_2\) with no free surface current.
  3. If \(\mathbf K_f=K\hat{\mathbf y}\), find \(B_{2x}\) in terms of \(K,\mu_1,\mu_2,B_{1x}\).
  4. Explain why the jump in \(H_t\) depends on free rather than total currents.
Reveal worked solution · Parts (a)–(d)

(a) Derive magnetic jump conditions

Gauss's law for magnetism \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0\), applied to a pillbox, yields

\[\boxed{\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot(\mathbf B_2-\mathbf B_1)=0},\quad B_{2z}=B_{1z}.\]

In magnetostatics, \(\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J_f\). A vanishing rectangular loop crossing a free sheet current gives

\[\boxed{\hat{\mathbf n}\times(\mathbf H_2-\mathbf H_1)=\mathbf K_f}.\]

(b) No sheet current

With \(\mathbf K_f=\mathbf0\), tangential \(H\) is continuous. Since \(H=B/\mu\):

\[H_{2x}=H_{1x}=\frac{0.60\ \mathrm T}{2\mu_0},\qquad B_{2x}=\mu_2H_{2x}=\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1}(0.60\ \mathrm T)=1.20\ \mathrm T.\]

Normal \(B\) remains \(0.80\ \mathrm T\):

\[\boxed{\mathbf B_2=(1.20\hat{\mathbf x}+0.80\hat{\mathbf z})\ \mathrm{T}}.\]

(c) Add free surface current

Let \(\mathbf K_f=K\hat{\mathbf y}\). Since \(\hat{\mathbf z}\times\hat{\mathbf x}=\hat{\mathbf y}\),

\[\hat{\mathbf z}\times(\mathbf H_2-\mathbf H_1) =(H_{2x}-H_{1x})\hat{\mathbf y}=K\hat{\mathbf y}.\]

Thus

\[H_{2x}=H_{1x}+K=\frac{B_{1x}}{\mu_1}+K,\] \[\boxed{B_{2x}=\mu_2\left(\frac{B_{1x}}{\mu_1}+K\right)},\qquad \boxed{B_{2z}=B_{1z}=0.80\ \mathrm T}.\]

(d) Free versus bound current

The macroscopic relation \(\mathbf B=\mu_0(\mathbf H+\mathbf M)\) incorporates the magnetization response. Maxwell's macroscopic law \(\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J_f\) therefore counts only free current. If instead using \(\nabla\times\mathbf B/\mu_0\), one must account for both bound and free currents.

SI check: \(\mathbf B\,[\mathrm{T}]\), \(\mathbf H,K\,[\mathrm{A/m}]\), \(\mu_1,\mu_2\,[\mathrm{H/m}]\). Each term \(B_{1x}/\mu_1+K\) has units \(\mathrm{A/m}\), and multiplying by \(\mu_2\) returns tesla.

SESSION C / PROBLEM 5 / 35 min / 10 POINTS

Uniformly magnetized sphere

QUESTION

A sphere of radius \(a\) in vacuum has prescribed spatially uniform magnetization \(\mathbf M=M_0\hat{\mathbf z}\). There are no free currents. In spherical coordinates, measure polar angle \(\theta\) from \(+\hat{\mathbf z}\).

  1. Find \(\mathbf J_b=\nabla\times\mathbf M\) and \(\mathbf K_b=\mathbf M\times\hat{\mathbf r}\).
  2. Explain the scalar potential \(\mathbf H=-\nabla\Phi_m\) and use the finite/decaying dipole forms \(\Phi_{\rm in}=Ar\cos\theta\), \(\Phi_{\rm out}=C\cos\theta/r^2\).
  3. Apply continuity of \(H_\theta\) and \(B_r\) at \(r=a\) to find \(A,C\).
  4. Determine \(\mathbf H_{\rm in}\), \(\mathbf B_{\rm in}\), and the equivalent external magnetic dipole moment \(\mathbf m\).
Reveal worked solution · Parts (a)–(d)

(a) Bound current densities

Uniform magnetization has zero curl, so

\[\boxed{\mathbf J_b=\nabla\times\mathbf M=\mathbf0}.\]

On the surface, \(\hat{\mathbf z}=\cos\theta\hat{\mathbf r}-\sin\theta\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}\). Thus

\[\mathbf K_b=\mathbf M\times\hat{\mathbf r} =M_0(\cos\theta\hat{\mathbf r}-\sin\theta\hat{\boldsymbol\theta})\times\hat{\mathbf r}.\]

Since \(\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}\times\hat{\mathbf r}=-\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\),

\[\boxed{\mathbf K_b=M_0\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}}.\]

(b) Scalar potential and Laplace equation

No free current means \(\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf0\); in the simply connected interior and exterior regions write \(\mathbf H=-\nabla\Phi_m\). Away from the surface, \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0\) and uniform \(\mathbf M\) give \(\nabla^2\Phi_m=0\). The axially symmetric \(\ell=1\) terms finite at the origin and decaying at infinity are

\[\boxed{\Phi_{\rm in}=Ar\cos\theta},\qquad \boxed{\Phi_{\rm out}=\frac{C\cos\theta}{r^2}}.\]

(c) Apply the two boundary conditions

Differentiate in spherical coordinates:

\[H_r=-\frac{\partial\Phi_m}{\partial r},\qquad H_\theta=-\frac1r\frac{\partial\Phi_m}{\partial\theta}.\]

Inside, \(H_{r,\rm in}=-A\cos\theta\), \(H_{\theta,\rm in}=A\sin\theta\). Outside, \(H_{r,\rm out}=2C\cos\theta/r^3\), \(H_{\theta,\rm out}=C\sin\theta/r^3\).

No free sheet current makes \(H_\theta\) continuous at \(r=a\):

\[A\sin\theta=\frac{C}{a^3}\sin\theta \quad\Rightarrow\quad C=Aa^3.\]

Normal \(\mathbf B\) is continuous, and inside \(\mathbf B=\mu_0(\mathbf H+\mathbf M)\):

\[\mu_0\frac{2C}{a^3}\cos\theta =\mu_0(-A+M_0)\cos\theta.\]

Substitute \(C=Aa^3\):

\[2A=-A+M_0\quad\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{A=\frac{M_0}{3}},\qquad \boxed{C=\frac{M_0a^3}{3}}.\]

(d) Uniform interior fields and external dipole moment

As \(r\cos\theta=z\), \(\Phi_{\rm in}=Az\), so

\[\boxed{\mathbf H_{\rm in}=-\frac{M_0}{3}\hat{\mathbf z}},\qquad \boxed{\mathbf B_{\rm in}=\mu_0(\mathbf H_{\rm in}+\mathbf M) =\frac{2\mu_0M_0}{3}\hat{\mathbf z}}.\]

The external magnetic scalar dipole potential is \(\Phi_{\rm out}=m\cos\theta/(4\pi r^2)\). Matching coefficients gives

\[\boxed{\mathbf m=4\pi C\hat{\mathbf z} =\frac{4\pi a^3}{3}M_0\hat{\mathbf z} =V_{\rm sphere}\mathbf M}.\]

Demagnetizing factor for a uniformly magnetized sphere: \(\boxed{N_d=1/3}\). The demagnetizing field is antiparallel to \(\mathbf M\).

SI check: \(M_0,\mathbf H\,[\mathrm{A/m}]\), \(\mathbf B\,[\mathrm T]\), \(a\,[\mathrm m]\), \(\Phi_m\,[\mathrm A]\), \(A\,[\mathrm{A/m}]\), \(C,\mathbf m\,[\mathrm{A\,m^2}]\), \(\mathbf K_b\,[\mathrm{A/m}]\), \(\mathbf J_b\,[\mathrm{A/m^2}]\). The demagnetizing factor is dimensionless.

SESSION D / PROBLEM 6 / 30 min / 10 POINTS

Plane electromagnetic wave in a dielectric

QUESTION

A homogeneous, lossless, nondispersive dielectric has permittivity \(\varepsilon\), permeability \(\mu\), no free charge or current, and an electric wave propagating along \(+\hat{\mathbf z}\):

\[\mathbf E(z,t)=E_0\cos(kz-\omega t)\hat{\mathbf x}.\]
  1. Derive the electric wave equation from Maxwell's curl laws.
  2. Find \(v,\eta,\mathbf B,\mathbf H\) and their directions.
  3. Derive the cycle-average Poynting vector.
  4. For \(\varepsilon=4\varepsilon_0,\mu=\mu_0,E_0=100\ \mathrm{V/m}\), vacuum wavelength \(\lambda_0=600\ \mathrm{nm}\), find the medium wavelength, \(B_0\), and mean intensity.
Reveal worked solution · Parts (a)–(d)

(a) Derive the wave equation

In the source-free uniform medium,

\[\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\mu\frac{\partial\mathbf H}{\partial t},\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf H=\varepsilon\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}.\]

Curl Faraday's law and substitute Ampère–Maxwell:

\[\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf E) =-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla\times\mathbf H) =-\mu\varepsilon\frac{\partial^2\mathbf E}{\partial t^2}.\]

Use \(\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf E)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)-\nabla^2\mathbf E\) and \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0\):

\[\boxed{\nabla^2\mathbf E-\mu\varepsilon\frac{\partial^2\mathbf E}{\partial t^2}=\mathbf0}.\]

For the harmonic plane wave \(k^2=\mu\varepsilon\omega^2\), hence

\[\boxed{v=\frac\omega k=\frac1{\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}}},\qquad \boxed{\eta=\sqrt{\frac\mu\varepsilon}=\mu v}.\]

(b) Find the magnetic field directions

For \(\mathbf E=E_x(z,t)\hat{\mathbf x}\), its curl has the \(y\)-component \(\partial_zE_x=-kE_0\sin(kz-\omega t)\). Faraday's law therefore requires \(\mathbf B\parallel+\hat{\mathbf y}\) and \(B_0=E_0/v\).

\[\boxed{\mathbf B(z,t)=\frac{E_0}{v}\cos(kz-\omega t)\hat{\mathbf y}},\] \[\boxed{\mathbf H(z,t)=\frac{E_0}{\eta}\cos(kz-\omega t)\hat{\mathbf y}}.\]

Since \(\hat{\mathbf x}\times\hat{\mathbf y}=\hat{\mathbf z}\), energy propagates along \(+\hat{\mathbf z}\).

(c) Average power flow

\[\mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H =\frac{E_0^2}{\eta}\cos^2(kz-\omega t)\hat{\mathbf z}.\]

As \(\langle\cos^2\rangle=1/2\),

\[\boxed{\langle\mathbf S\rangle=\frac{E_0^2}{2\eta}\hat{\mathbf z}}.\]

(d) Numerical example

With \(\varepsilon_r=4\) and \(\mu_r=1\),

\[n=\sqrt{\varepsilon_r\mu_r}=2,\quad \boxed{v=\frac c2\approx1.50\times10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}},\] \[\boxed{\eta=\frac{\eta_0}{2}\approx188.5\ \Omega}.\]

Frequency is unchanged at the interface, so

\[\boxed{\lambda=\frac{\lambda_0}{n} =\frac{600\ \mathrm{nm}}{2}=300\ \mathrm{nm}}.\]

The magnetic amplitude is

\[\boxed{B_0=\frac{100\ \mathrm{V/m}}{1.50\times10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}} =6.67\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm T}.\]

And mean intensity is

\[\boxed{\langle S\rangle=\frac{(100\ \mathrm{V/m})^2}{2(188.5\ \Omega)} \approx26.5\ \mathrm{W/m^2}}.\]

SI check: \(E_0\,[\mathrm{V/m}]\), \(B_0\,[\mathrm T]\), \(H_0\,[\mathrm{A/m}]\), \(\lambda\,[\mathrm m]\), \(v\,[\mathrm{m/s}]\), \(\eta\,[\Omega]\), and energy flux \(\langle\mathbf S\rangle\,[\mathrm{W/m^2}]\).

SESSION D / PROBLEM 7 / 30 min / 10 POINTS

Normal-incidence Fresnel coefficients

QUESTION

A monochromatic plane wave is normally incident from vacuum (medium 1) onto a lossless nonmagnetic dielectric (medium 2) with \(\varepsilon_{r2}=4\). The interface is \(z=0\); the incident wave propagates along \(+\hat{\mathbf z}\) and is \(\hat{\mathbf x}\)-polarized. There is no free surface current.

  1. Write incident, reflected and transmitted electric fields in terms of amplitudes \(E_i,E_r,E_t\), and state the magnetic directions.
  2. Apply tangential \(\mathbf E,\mathbf H\) continuity to derive \(r=E_r/E_i\) and \(t=E_t/E_i\) in terms of \(\eta_1,\eta_2\).
  3. For \(n_1=1,n_2=2\), calculate \(r,t,R,T\).
  4. Check energy conservation and interpret the negative \(r\).
Reveal worked solution · Parts (a)–(d)

(a) Field phasors and propagation directions

Use the time convention \(e^{-i\omega t}\). The electric phasors are

\[\widetilde{\mathbf E}_i=E_ie^{ik_1z}\hat{\mathbf x},\quad \widetilde{\mathbf E}_r=E_re^{-ik_1z}\hat{\mathbf x},\quad \widetilde{\mathbf E}_t=E_te^{ik_2z}\hat{\mathbf x}.\]

For each wave, \(\widetilde{\mathbf H}=\hat{\mathbf k}\times\widetilde{\mathbf E}/\eta\). Propagation directions are \(+\hat{\mathbf z},-\hat{\mathbf z},+\hat{\mathbf z}\), so

\[\widetilde{\mathbf H}_i=\frac{E_i}{\eta_1}e^{ik_1z}\hat{\mathbf y},\quad \widetilde{\mathbf H}_r=-\frac{E_r}{\eta_1}e^{-ik_1z}\hat{\mathbf y},\quad \widetilde{\mathbf H}_t=\frac{E_t}{\eta_2}e^{ik_2z}\hat{\mathbf y}.\]

(b) Impose Maxwell's tangential boundary conditions

At \(z=0\), tangential electric-field continuity gives

\[E_i+E_r=E_t.\]

With no free sheet current, tangential magnetic-field continuity gives

\[\frac{E_i-E_r}{\eta_1}=\frac{E_t}{\eta_2}.\]

Define \(r=E_r/E_i\), \(t=E_t/E_i\). Dividing by \(E_i\) produces \(1+r=t\) and \((1-r)/\eta_1=t/\eta_2\). Solve:

\[\boxed{r=\frac{\eta_2-\eta_1}{\eta_2+\eta_1}},\qquad \boxed{t=\frac{2\eta_2}{\eta_1+\eta_2}}.\]

(c) Numerical reflection and transmission

For nonmagnetic media, \(\eta=\eta_0/n\). Thus \(\eta_1=\eta_0\), \(\eta_2=\eta_0/2\):

\[\boxed{r=-\frac13},\qquad\boxed{t=\frac23}.\]

Power fractions are not simply the field-amplitude fractions. With real positive impedances,

\[\boxed{R=|r|^2=\frac19\approx11.1\%},\] \[\boxed{T=\frac{\eta_1}{\eta_2}|t|^2 =2\cdot\frac49=\frac89\approx88.9\%}.\]

(d) Conservation and phase

\[\boxed{R+T=\frac19+\frac89=1}.\]

The negative \(r\) means a phase reversal of \(\pi\) for the reflected electric field relative to the incident field at the interface. Reflected power is nonnegative.

SI check: \(\eta_1,\eta_2\,[\Omega]\), \(E_i,E_r,E_t\,[\mathrm{V/m}]\). The ratios \(r,t,R,T\) are dimensionless, as are percentages.

SESSION D / PROBLEM 8 / 35 min / 10 POINTS

Skin depth in a good conductor

QUESTION

A monochromatic plane wave enters a thick homogeneous Ohmic conductor in the \(+\hat{\mathbf z}\) direction. The material satisfies \(\mathbf D=\varepsilon\mathbf E\), \(\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf H\), \(\mathbf J=\sigma\mathbf E\), with no free volume charge. Use harmonic dependence \(e^{-i\omega t}\).

  1. Derive the damped electric-field wave equation.
  2. With \(\mathbf E\propto e^{i(kz-\omega t)}\), find the complex dispersion \(k^2\).
  3. For a good conductor, \(\sigma\gg\omega\varepsilon\), show \(k\approx(1+i)/\delta\), \(\delta=\sqrt{2/(\omega\mu\sigma)}\); interpret \(\delta\).
  4. For copper with \(\sigma=5.8\times10^7\ \mathrm{S/m}\), \(\mu=\mu_0\), and \(f=1.0\ \mathrm{MHz}\), find \(\delta\) and the depth where average power is 1% of its surface value.
Reveal worked solution · Parts (a)–(d)

(a) Derive the conducting-medium wave equation

Maxwell's curl equations and Ohm's law read

\[\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\mu\frac{\partial\mathbf H}{\partial t},\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf H=\sigma\mathbf E+\varepsilon\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}.\]

Take the curl of Faraday's law:

\[\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf E) =-\mu\sigma\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t} -\mu\varepsilon\frac{\partial^2\mathbf E}{\partial t^2}.\]

As the medium is uniform and charge-free, \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0\). The identity \(\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf E)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)-\nabla^2\mathbf E\) yields

\[\boxed{\nabla^2\mathbf E-\mu\varepsilon\frac{\partial^2\mathbf E}{\partial t^2} -\mu\sigma\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}=\mathbf0}.\]

(b) Complex wavenumber

For \(\mathbf E\propto e^{i(kz-\omega t)}\), the operators act as \(\partial_t\to-i\omega\), \(\partial_t^2\to-\omega^2\), and \(\partial_z^2\to-k^2\). Substitute:

\[-k^2+\mu\varepsilon\omega^2+i\mu\sigma\omega=0,\] \[\boxed{k^2=\mu\varepsilon\omega^2+i\mu\sigma\omega}.\]

(c) Good-conductor approximation and amplitude decay

If \(\sigma\gg\omega\varepsilon\), the Ohmic term dominates: \(k^2\simeq i\mu\sigma\omega\). Since \(\sqrt{i}=(1+i)/\sqrt2\), choose the root with positive imaginary part for decay into \(z>0\):

\[\boxed{k\simeq(1+i)\sqrt{\frac{\omega\mu\sigma}{2}} =\frac{1+i}{\delta}},\qquad \boxed{\delta=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega\mu\sigma}}}.\]

Then \(e^{ikz}=e^{iz/\delta}e^{-z/\delta}\): field amplitude falls by \(1/e\) at \(z=\delta\). Time-averaged power flux instead decreases as \(e^{-2z/\delta}\).

(d) Copper example and 1% power depth

Use \(\omega=2\pi f=2\pi\times10^6\ \mathrm{rad/s}\), \(\mu=\mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{H/m}\), and \(\sigma=5.8\times10^7\ \mathrm{S/m}\):

\[\delta=\sqrt{\frac{2}{(2\pi\times10^6)(4\pi\times10^{-7})(5.8\times10^7)}}.\] \[\boxed{\delta\approx6.61\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm m=66.1\ \mu\mathrm m}.\]

To find the depth \(z\) where the *transmitted surface power* has fallen to 1%,

\[\frac{\langle S(z)\rangle}{\langle S(0)\rangle} =e^{-2z/\delta}=0.01.\]

Taking logarithms,

\[-\frac{2z}{\delta}=\ln(0.01)=-\ln(100) \quad\Rightarrow\quad z=\frac{\delta}{2}\ln(100).\] \[\boxed{z\approx2.302585\,\delta\approx1.52\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm m =152\ \mu\mathrm m}.\]

This is relative to power just inside the conductor, not the initial incident power before interface reflection. Doubling frequency decreases skin depth by \(1/\sqrt2\) if \(\mu,\sigma\) remain unchanged.

SI check: conductivity \(\sigma\,[\mathrm{S/m}]\), frequency \(f\,[\mathrm{Hz}]\), \(\omega\,[\mathrm{rad/s}]\), \(\mu\,[\mathrm{H/m}]\), \(k\,[\mathrm{m^{-1}}]\), \(\delta,z\,[\mathrm m]\), and power flux \(\langle S\rangle\,[\mathrm{W/m^2}]\). The ratio of powers is dimensionless.

AFTER THE PROBLEM SET / ORAL-EXAM CHECKS

Eight rapid conceptual checks

These correspond to the final page of the uploaded worksheet. Read each statement and explain why it holds before expanding the answer.

1. Changing electric fields and circulating magnetic fields

Even when no conduction current crosses a chosen surface, time-varying electric flux contributes to Maxwell–Ampère's law through \(\varepsilon_0\,d\Phi_E/dt\). That contribution produces a magnetic-field circulation.

2. Normal electric fields at dielectric interfaces

Without free surface charge, \(D_n\) is continuous, but \(E_n=D_n/\varepsilon\) generally changes when permittivity changes. Electrostatic \(E_t\) is continuous.

3. Normal magnetic fields at material interfaces

\(B_n\) is continuous because \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0\); if no free surface current is present, \(H_t\) is continuous. \(B_t=\mu H_t\) can still change across a permeability discontinuity.

4. Magnetization and bound currents

\(\nabla\times\mathbf B/\mu_0\) responds to total current; the macroscopic form \(\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J_f\) incorporates bound-current response in \(\mathbf M\) and explicitly uses free current.

5. Wave speed versus impedance

\(v=1/\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}\,[\mathrm{m/s}]\), while \(\eta=\sqrt{\mu/\varepsilon}\,[\Omega]\). These are different physical quantities, even though both depend on \(\varepsilon,\mu\).

6. Negative reflection coefficient and energy conservation

A negative \(r\) indicates a reflected electric field phase reversal, not negative power. At normal incidence into lossless media, \(R=|r|^2\) and \(T=(\eta_1/\eta_2)|t|^2\); they satisfy \(R+T=1\).

7. Skin depth and power decay

The skin depth \(\delta\) is the \(1/e\) field-amplitude attenuation length. Under the local plane-wave approximation, power decays as \(e^{-2z/\delta}\); its \(1/e\) depth is \(\delta/2\).

8. Interpreting \(\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf E\) in Poynting's theorem

If \(\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf E>0\), work is being done by fields on matter. In a passive Ohmic conductor this is dissipated as heat. Hence the field-energy balance contains \(-\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf E\).
